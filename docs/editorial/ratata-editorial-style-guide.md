# ratata Editorial Style Guide

This guide defines how ratata writes in UK English and German across its website, exhibition pages, event announcements and product texts. It is a working standard: consistent enough to make the voice recognisable, flexible enough to let each project retain its own character.

Dieser Guide definiert, wie ratata auf der Website sowie in Texten zu Ausstellungen, Events und Produkten in britischem Englisch und Deutsch kommuniziert. Er ist ein Arbeitsstandard: konsistent genug für eine wiedererkennbare Stimme, flexibel genug für den eigenen Charakter jedes Projekts.

## 1. Editorial identity / Redaktionelle Identität

### 1.1 The editorial idea / Die redaktionelle Idee

**EN**

ratata presents contemporary and digital art with precision, curiosity and a controlled sense of play. The writing opens a door into the work: it gives readers enough context to understand what they are seeing without explaining away its ambiguity.

**DE**

ratata vermittelt zeitgenössische und digitale Kunst präzise, neugierig und mit einer kontrollierten Portion Spielfreude. Die Texte öffnen einen Zugang zu den Arbeiten: Sie geben genügend Kontext, ohne ihre Mehrdeutigkeit wegzuerklären.

**Core promise / Redaktionsversprechen**

> Clear enough to enter. Open enough to explore.  
> Klar genug für den Einstieg. Offen genug zum Entdecken.

### 1.2 Brand styling and point of view / Markenschreibweise und Perspektive

- Write the editorial name as **ratata**, in lower case. In English, use **ratata’s** for the possessive; in German, use **ratatas** without an apostrophe.
- Avoid beginning a sentence with the name where the lower-case opening feels distracting. Restructure the sentence instead.
- Use **we / wir** selectively for a direct statement from ratata: “We invited six artists…” / „Wir haben sechs Künstler:innen eingeladen …“
- Prefer an objective third-person voice for archival facts, credits and institutional context.
- Address readers only when an action is useful. Use **you** in English and lower-case **du / dich / dein** in German. Avoid switching between **du**, **Sie** and impersonal forms on the same page.
- Do not speak for artists. Attribute interpretations, intentions and quotations when they come from an artist or curator.

### 1.3 Voice principles / Prinzipien der Stimme

| Principle | Write like this | Avoid this | Deutsches Prinzip |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clear before clever | Name the work, idea or action early. | Making the reader decode the first sentence. | Verständlichkeit vor Wortspiel |
| Informed, not academic | Give the context needed to see more. | Theory used as decoration or proof of status. | Kenntnisreich, nicht akademisch |
| Experimental, not chaotic | Let structure hold unusual ideas. | Random punctuation, unexplained jargon or visual noise. | Experimentell, nicht beliebig |
| Confident, not inflated | State what happened and why it matters. | “Groundbreaking”, “iconic” or “revolutionary” without evidence. | Selbstbewusst, nicht überhöht |
| Warm, not promotional | Invite attention and participation. | Sales pressure, manufactured urgency or generic excitement. | Zugewandt, nicht werblich |
| Playful with purpose | Use wit when it belongs to the project. | Forcing jokes into practical or sensitive information. | Spielerisch mit Absicht |

### 1.4 Tone by context / Tonalität nach Kontext

The voice remains stable; the tone changes with the situation.

Die Stimme bleibt stabil; die Tonalität passt sich der Situation an.

| Context | Tone | Editorial balance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exhibition / Ausstellung | Observant, precise, lightly evocative / Beobachtend, präzise, leicht atmosphärisch | Lead with the curatorial idea; support it with concrete works, media, people and context. |
| Event | Immediate, welcoming, practical / Direkt, einladend, praktisch | Tell readers what will happen, why they might care and how to take part. |
| Product / Produkt | Tactile, specific, transparent / Anschaulich, konkret, transparent | Describe the object or digital work before creating desire; make all specifications easy to find. |
| Archive / Archiv | Factual, concise, contextual / Sachlich, kompakt, einordnend | Use past tense and preserve names, dates, roles and venue accurately. |
| Service information / Serviceinformation | Calm, direct, unambiguous / Ruhig, direkt, eindeutig | Prioritise access, timing, cost, availability and required action over personality. |

### 1.5 What ratata sounds like / So klingt ratata

**Prefer / Bevorzugen**

- specific nouns and active verbs;
- short openings followed by richer context;
- concrete details about works, materials, participants, place and time;
- a rhythm that combines short and medium-length sentences;
- one clear idea per paragraph;
- curiosity without mystification;
- humour that comes from the project, title or observation.

**Avoid / Vermeiden**

- generic openings such as “We are thrilled to announce…” / „Wir freuen uns riesig, bekannt zu geben …“;
- unsupported superlatives: “unique”, “unmissable”, “world-class”, “groundbreaking” / „einzigartig“, „bahnbrechend“, „sensationell“;
- empty art language: “explores the intersection of…”, “pushes the boundaries of…” / „bewegt sich an der Schnittstelle von …“, „sprengt die Grenzen von …“;
- crypto and tech hype: “the future of art”, “next-generation”, “game-changing”, “must-have drop”;
- false scarcity or pressure: “Only today”, “Don’t miss out”, “Act now” unless the statement is literally true and necessary;
- interpreting an artist’s identity, biography or work more strongly than the available source supports.

### 1.6 Editorial vocabulary / Redaktionelle Wortwahl

Use the most precise term for the role, format or action. Do not vary terminology merely to avoid repetition.

Verwendet den präzisesten Begriff für Rolle, Format oder Handlung. Begriffe sollten nicht allein deshalb wechseln, um Wiederholungen zu vermeiden.

| Meaning | UK English | Deutsch | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A public presentation of art | exhibition | Ausstellung | Use “show” / „Schau“ sparingly in informal copy. |
| A single artistic item | work or artwork | Arbeit or Kunstwerk | “Work” / „Arbeit“ is usually less promotional. |
| Person making art | artist | Künstler:in | Prefer neutral plurals such as „Kunstschaffende“ where natural; do not overuse them. |
| Organised the artistic concept | curated by | kuratiert von | Do not substitute “organised” if the role was curatorial. |
| Managed the realisation | produced by | realisiert von / produziert von | Choose the German term that reflects the actual role. |
| Made the event happen | organised by | organisiert von / veranstaltet von | “Hosted by” is not always a curatorial credit. |
| Available for viewing | on view | zu sehen | For current or upcoming exhibitions. |
| Public first evening | opening | Eröffnung | Use “vernissage” / „Vernissage“ only when it is the event’s chosen name. |
| Artist-led live work | performance | Performance | Add duration or start time when known. |
| Moderated conversation | talk / conversation | Gespräch / Podiumsgespräch | Prefer the specific format over generic “event”. |
| Numbered physical release | edition | Edition / Auflage | State size, artist’s proofs and signature status separately. |
| Digital edition | digital edition | digitale Edition | Add platform, chain or file details only when they help the buyer. |
| Release of a product or edition | release / launch | Veröffentlichung / erscheint am | Use “drop” only if it is part of the project’s deliberate language. |
| Non-fungible token | NFT | NFT | Explain its practical relevance; do not use it as a synonym for the artwork. |

## 2. Language and formatting / Sprache und Formatierung

### 2.1 Bilingual writing is adaptation, not duplication / Zweisprachiges Schreiben bedeutet Adaption

English and German versions must contain the same facts, but they do not need the same syntax, rhythm or sentence count. Finish the source text, then rewrite it naturally in the second language. Do not translate unfinished copy line by line.

Die englische und die deutsche Fassung müssen dieselben Fakten enthalten, aber nicht dieselbe Syntax, denselben Rhythmus oder dieselbe Satzanzahl. Zuerst wird der Ausgangstext fertiggestellt, anschließend idiomatisch übertragen. Unfertige Texte werden nicht Satz für Satz übersetzt.

**Keep identical / Identisch halten**

- names and preferred spellings of artists, collectives, institutions and venues;
- official titles of exhibitions, works, series, products and events;
- dates, times, prices, edition sizes, dimensions and credits;
- URLs, availability and access information;
- the meaning and strength of every claim.

**Adapt / Anpassen**

- sentence structure and paragraph rhythm;
- idioms, verbs and calls to action;
- explanatory context for unfamiliar terms;
- punctuation, date formats, time formats and number formats;
- gender-inclusive and accessible language.

**Common adaptations / Häufige Übertragungen**

| Avoid literal wording | Prefer in context |
| --- | --- |
| “The exhibition features 14 artists.” → „Die Ausstellung featured 14 Artists.“ | „Die Ausstellung vereint Arbeiten von 14 Künstler:innen.“ |
| “Join us for an artist talk.” → „Joine uns für einen Artist Talk.“ | „Kommt zu einem Gespräch mit der Künstlerin / dem Künstler.“ |
| “The work is on view.” → „Die Arbeit ist auf Sicht.“ | „Die Arbeit ist zu sehen.“ |
| „Die Edition erscheint am Freitag.“ → “The edition appears on Friday.” | “The edition launches on Friday.” |
| „Der Eintritt ist frei.“ → “The entrance is free.” | “Admission is free.” |

### 2.2 UK English house rules

- Use UK spelling: **colour, centre, organise, travelling, programme**.
- Distinguish **practice** (noun) and **practise** (verb), **licence** (noun) and **license** (verb).
- Use **artwork**, **website**, **email** and **online** without a hyphen.
- Use sentence case for headings: “A shared space for digital art”, not “A Shared Space for Digital Art”.
- Use single quotation marks for quotations: ‘…’; use double quotation marks only for a quotation within a quotation.
- Use the serial comma only when it prevents ambiguity.
- Prefer active constructions: “ratata curated the exhibition”, not “The exhibition was curated by ratata”, unless the exhibition is the deliberate focus.
- Avoid contractions in formal archive or product specifications. They are acceptable in short, conversational event copy.
- Italicise titles of exhibitions, artworks, publications and films in running text. Do not italicise titles in page headings, menus, metadata or lists.
- Preserve the official capitalisation and punctuation of a title, even when it differs from house style: *What hot Sh!t*.

### 2.3 Deutsche Sprachregeln

- Überschriften folgen der deutschen Groß- und Kleinschreibung; es wird keine englische Title Case übernommen.
- Der Doppelpunkt markiert Personenbezeichnungen, wenn eine neutrale Form nicht natürlich funktioniert: **Künstler:innen**, **Teilnehmer:innen**, **Besucher:innen**.
- Neutrale Begriffe sind willkommen, wenn sie klar und geläufig sind: **Kunstschaffende**, **Publikum**, **Team**, **Gäste**. Ungewohnte Partizipformen werden nicht erzwungen.
- Direkte Ansprache verwendet **du / dich / dein** in Kleinschreibung. Bei Gruppen ist **ihr / euch / euer** möglich. Innerhalb eines Textes bleibt die Ansprache konsistent.
- Anglizismen werden nur verwendet, wenn sie im Kunstkontext etabliert, Teil eines Titels oder genauer als die deutsche Alternative sind. Bevorzugt werden **Eröffnung** statt “Opening”, **Gespräch** statt “Talk” und **Veröffentlichung** statt “Drop”.
- Zusammensetzungen werden korrekt verbunden: **Online-Ausstellung**, **Live-Performance**, **Pop-up-Ausstellung**, **NFT-basierte Arbeit**. Eigennamen bleiben unverändert.
- Ausstellungs-, Werk-, Publikations- und Filmtitel werden im Fließtext kursiv gesetzt, nicht aber in Überschriften, Navigation, Metadaten oder Listen.
- ratata erhält im Genitiv kein Apostroph: **ratatas Programm**, nicht **ratata’s Programm**.
- Vermeidet unnötiges Passiv und Nominalstil: „ratata zeigt sechs neue Arbeiten“ statt „Die Präsentation von sechs neuen Arbeiten wird durch ratata vorgenommen“.

### 2.4 Sentences, paragraphs and hierarchy / Sätze, Absätze und Hierarchie

- Aim for 12–22 words per sentence in English and 10–20 words in German on average. Longer sentences are welcome when their structure remains clear.
- Keep web paragraphs to two to four sentences. A one-sentence paragraph may be used for emphasis, not as the default.
- The first paragraph must work without the reader seeing the hero image.
- Put the key idea or action in the first two sentences. Background follows.
- Use one H1 per page. The page title or frontmatter normally supplies it.
- Use H2 headings for major sections and H3 headings only when the page genuinely needs another level.
- Headings should label content, not tease it. Prefer “Artists and works” / „Künstler:innen und Arbeiten“ over “Discover more”.
- Do not end headings, captions, labels or buttons with a full stop.
- Avoid a series of one-line fragments. Use fragments only in metadata, captions or a deliberate campaign line.

### 2.5 Punctuation and symbols / Zeichensetzung und Symbole

| Element | UK English | Deutsch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parenthetical dash | spaced en dash: “The work – first shown in 2021 – returns.” | Gedankenstrich mit Leerzeichen: „Die Arbeit – erstmals 2021 gezeigt – kehrt zurück.“ |
| Range | en dash without spaces: “9 July–6 August” | Halbgeviertstrich ohne Leerzeichen: „9. Juli–6. August“ |
| Quotation marks | ‘single quotation marks’ | „deutsche Anführungszeichen“ |
| Dimensions | 30 × 40 cm | 30 × 40 cm |
| Slash | Avoid for prose; use only for true alternatives or fixed names. | Im Fließtext vermeiden; nur für echte Alternativen oder feste Bezeichnungen. |
| Ampersand | Only in official names, titles or compact navigation. | Nur in offiziellen Namen, Titeln oder kompakter Navigation. |
| Exclamation mark | Maximum one; usually none. | Höchstens eines; meistens keines. |

Do not use repeated exclamation marks, spaced ellipses, decorative slashes or all caps to manufacture energy. A project title may deliberately break these rules.

Keine mehrfachen Ausrufezeichen, auseinandergezogenen Auslassungspunkte, dekorativen Schrägstriche oder Versalien verwenden, um künstlich Energie zu erzeugen. Ein Projekttitel darf diese Regeln bewusst brechen.

### 2.6 Dates, times, numbers, prices and places / Datum, Uhrzeit, Zahlen, Preise und Orte

| Information | UK English | Deutsch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single date | 9 July 2021 | 9. Juli 2021 |
| Date range, different months | 9 July–6 August 2021 | 9. Juli–6. August 2021 |
| Date range, same month | 9–16 July 2021 | 9.–16. Juli 2021 |
| Time | 7 pm; 7.30 pm | 19 Uhr; 19:30 Uhr |
| Time range | 7–9 pm | 19–21 Uhr |
| Price | €25; €25.50 | 25 €; 25,50 € |
| Free entry | Admission is free | Eintritt frei |
| Venue line | Galerie Greulich, Frankfurt | Galerie Greulich, Frankfurt |

- Do not use ordinal endings in English dates: 9 July, not 9th July.
- Use a 12-hour clock in English editorial copy and a 24-hour clock in German. Add the time zone for online or international events.
- In English prose, spell out one to nine and use numerals from 10. Use numerals for dates, times, prices, dimensions, edition sizes and technical specifications.
- In German prose, spell out eins to zwölf when it reads naturally; use numerals for compact factual information and all specifications.
- Pair the venue with the city on first reference. Add the full street address in practical information, not in the opening paragraph.
- Use ISO dates (`YYYY-MM-DD`) in machine-readable frontmatter and localised dates in visible copy.

### 2.7 Names, titles, credits and artist lists / Namen, Titel, Credits und Künstler:innenlisten

- Confirm every person’s preferred name, diacritics, pronouns and role from an authoritative source.
- Do not translate official titles unless an official translated title exists. If a translation is useful, give it once in parentheses.
- Credit distinct roles accurately: **curated by**, **produced by**, **organised by**, **presented by**, **in partnership with**, **supported by**.
- Keep credit order consistent across both languages.
- List up to five artists in a sentence when the sentence remains readable. For six or more, use a separate list.
- Alphabetise long artist lists by surname unless the curatorial concept or an agreed credit order requires another sequence.
- Do not use “including” / „darunter“ if every participant is named.
- Do not call a group exhibition a collaboration unless the artists actually collaborated.

### 2.8 Links, calls to action and interface copy / Links, Handlungsaufforderungen und Interface-Texte

Buttons and links begin with a clear verb and describe the destination or action.

Buttons und Links beginnen mit einem klaren Verb und benennen Ziel oder Handlung.

| Purpose | UK English | Deutsch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exhibition page | View the exhibition | Ausstellung ansehen |
| Event registration | Book a place | Platz reservieren |
| Product detail | View the edition | Edition ansehen |
| Purchase | Add to basket | In den Warenkorb |
| Background article | Read the story | Geschichte lesen |
| External artist page | Visit [Artist]’s website | Website von [Name] besuchen |
| General expansion | Read more | Mehr erfahren |

Avoid vague labels such as “Click here” / „Hier klicken“. Never use “Book now” if there is no booking step, or “Buy now” if the next page only provides information.

### 2.9 Accessibility, inclusion and sensitive content / Barrierearmut, Inklusion und sensible Inhalte

- Describe access facts plainly: step-free entrance, lift, seating, toilets, interpretation, captions, language and duration. Never infer accessibility; publish only confirmed information.
- Announce strobe lighting, haze, loud sound, explicit imagery or other relevant content before the visit or purchase.
- Write image alt text for purpose, not inventory. Describe what matters in the image and avoid repeating the caption.
- Do not begin alt text with “Image of” / „Bild von“. Screen readers already identify the element as an image.
- For installation views, name the exhibition or artist when useful, then describe the spatial relationship, medium or action.
- Do not mention ethnicity, gender, disability, age or nationality unless it is relevant, accurate and handled with the person’s preferred language.
- Use singular **they** in English when a person’s pronouns are unknown. In German, restructure the sentence instead of guessing.
- Avoid metaphors that equate disability or mental illness with failure, chaos or danger.

**Alt-text example / Beispiel für Alt-Text**

- EN: “Three projected works cast blue and orange light across the darkened gallery; visitors stand between the screens.”
- DE: „Drei projizierte Arbeiten tauchen den abgedunkelten Galerieraum in blaues und oranges Licht; Besucher:innen stehen zwischen den Leinwänden.“

### 2.10 Markdown and MDX conventions / Markdown- und MDX-Konventionen

- Keep editorial copy in Markdown wherever possible. Use MDX components only when they add a defined function.
- Do not duplicate the page title as an H1 when the template already renders `title` from frontmatter.
- Use blank lines around headings, lists, blockquotes and code blocks.
- Use descriptive link text: `[Read the interview](…)`, not `[here](…)`.
- Keep captions separate from alt text. Alt text explains the image; a caption adds credit, title, year, medium or context.
- Use one language per page or locale file. Avoid alternating complete English and German paragraphs in public-facing body copy unless the design explicitly calls for it.
- Keep shared factual metadata synchronised between locale versions.
- Use plain UTF-8 characters: curly quotation marks in visible copy, an en dash for ranges and the multiplication sign `×` for dimensions.

## 3. Content types, templates and quality control / Inhaltstypen, Vorlagen und Qualitätskontrolle

### 3.1 Exhibitions / Ausstellungen

#### Editorial purpose / Redaktionelles Ziel

An exhibition text should help a reader understand what brings the works together, what form the exhibition takes and why this presentation matters here and now. It is not a complete catalogue essay.

Ein Ausstellungstext soll verständlich machen, was die Arbeiten verbindet, welche Form die Ausstellung annimmt und warum diese Präsentation an diesem Ort und zu diesem Zeitpunkt relevant ist. Er ist kein vollständiger Katalogessay.

#### Recommended structure / Empfohlene Struktur

1. **Label and title / Kennzeichnung und Titel**  
   Use “Exhibition” / „Ausstellung“ as an eyebrow if the design needs it. Preserve the official title exactly.
2. **Standfirst / Teaser**  
   One sentence, usually 18–30 words in English or 15–28 words in German. Name the central proposition rather than repeating dates.
3. **Opening paragraph / Einleitungsabsatz**  
   Roughly 50–90 words. Identify the format, central idea, participants and relevant context.
4. **Body / Haupttext**  
   Roughly 120–300 words for a standard project page. Move from idea to specific works, media, relationships or spatial decisions.
5. **Practical details / Praktische Angaben**  
   Dates, venue, city, opening hours, admission, access and current/archive status.
6. **Artists and credits / Künstler:innen und Credits**  
   Complete and role-specific. Separate curatorial, production, partner and funding credits.
7. **Media / Medien**  
   Captions include artist, title, year, medium and photo credit where available.

#### Writing rules / Schreibregeln

- Open with the exhibition’s proposition, not a generic celebration.
- Use present tense for a current exhibition, future or present tense for an upcoming exhibition and past tense for an archive page.
- Name media and formal decisions when they help readers picture the show.
- Explain a specialist term once if it is essential.
- Let interpretation emerge from concrete details; do not stack abstract nouns.
- Distinguish between works created for the exhibition and works newly presented in that context.
- Treat technology as a medium or condition, not as proof of innovation.
- Keep biographical information selective. Include only what helps readers understand the work or programme.

#### Example: archive copy / Beispiel: Archivtext

Based on the confirmed project information supplied for the website:

Auf Grundlage der für die Website bestätigten Projektinformationen:

**UK English**

> *What hot Sh!t* was the first hic et nunc exhibition in Frankfurt. Curated and produced by ratata, it brought together works by 14 international artists, including Mario Klingemann and Ivona Tau. The exhibition was on view at Galerie Greulich from 9 July to 6 August 2021.

**Deutsch**

> *What hot Sh!t* war die erste Ausstellung von hic et nunc in Frankfurt. Kuratiert und realisiert von ratata, vereinte sie Arbeiten von 14 internationalen Künstler:innen, darunter Mario Klingemann und Ivona Tau. Die Ausstellung war vom 9. Juli bis 6. August 2021 in der Galerie Greulich zu sehen.

**Why it works / Warum der Text funktioniert**

- It establishes the project’s historical context immediately.
- It names ratata’s role without overstatement.
- It uses one concrete scale marker and two representative artist names.
- It closes with venue and dates in the correct local format.

#### Avoid / Prefer

**Avoid**

> We are thrilled to present a groundbreaking exhibition featuring 14 amazing international artists who are redefining the future of NFT art.

> Wir freuen uns riesig, eine bahnbrechende Ausstellung mit 14 fantastischen internationalen Artists zu präsentieren, die die Zukunft der NFT-Kunst neu definieren.

**Prefer**

> The exhibition brings together 14 international artists working across generative systems, moving image and digital editions. Their works approach the network as material, meeting place and distribution system.

> Die Ausstellung vereint Arbeiten von 14 internationalen Künstler:innen aus den Bereichen generative Systeme, Bewegtbild und digitale Editionen. Sie begreifen das Netzwerk als Material, Begegnungsraum und Vertriebsstruktur.

Use this preferred example only when the named media and curatorial framing are factually accurate.

### 3.2 Events

#### Editorial purpose / Redaktionelles Ziel

Event copy turns interest into an informed decision: attend, register, watch or read later. It should answer **what, who, when, where, language, duration, cost and access** at a glance.

Eventtexte machen aus Interesse eine informierte Entscheidung: teilnehmen, reservieren, zuschauen oder später nachlesen. Sie beantworten **was, wer, wann, wo, Sprache, Dauer, Kosten und Zugänglichkeit** auf einen Blick.

#### Recommended structure / Empfohlene Struktur

1. Event type and title / Eventformat und Titel
2. One-sentence reason to attend / Ein Satz zum Anlass
3. Participants and format / Mitwirkende und Ablauf
4. Date, start time, duration, venue or stream / Datum, Beginn, Dauer, Ort oder Stream
5. Language and access / Sprache und Zugänglichkeit
6. Cost, capacity and booking status / Kosten, Kapazität und Reservierungsstatus
7. Accurate call to action / Zutreffende Handlungsaufforderung

#### Writing rules / Schreibregeln

- Begin with what will happen: conversation, performance, workshop, screening, tour or opening.
- Name speakers, performers and moderators with their actual roles.
- If the programme has several parts, show the running order rather than compressing it into prose.
- State whether doors and programme start at different times.
- For online events, state the time zone and platform before registration.
- Write “Admission is free; booking is required” / „Eintritt frei; Anmeldung erforderlich“ when both are true.
- Do not describe limited capacity as urgency. State the number or booking condition calmly.
- Update the page after the event: replace booking language with documentation, recording or archive status.

Das folgende Beispiel zeigt Ton und Struktur; alle Angaben in eckigen Klammern sind Platzhalter.

>

> [Ort, Stadt]  
> Auf [Deutsch/Englisch] · Stufenloser Zugang [falls bestätigt]

#### Avoid / Prefer

**Avoid**

> Join us for an unforgettable night of art, drinks and inspiring conversations. Don’t miss out!

> Sei dabei bei einer unvergesslichen Nacht voller Kunst, Drinks und inspirierender Gespräche. Nicht verpassen!

**Prefer**

### 3.3 Products and editions / Produkte und Editionen

#### Editorial purpose / Redaktionelles Ziel

Product copy should create understanding before desire. Readers need to know what the object or digital edition is, how it relates to ratata’s programme and exactly what they will receive.

Produkttexte schaffen zuerst Verständnis, dann Begehren. Leser:innen müssen erkennen, um welches Objekt oder welche digitale Edition es sich handelt, wie sie mit ratatas Programm verbunden ist und was genau sie erhalten.

#### The three layers / Die drei Ebenen

1. **Object / Objekt**  
   Name the product type, artist or designer, material or format and defining visual quality.
2. **Story / Kontext**  
   Explain the idea, collaboration or relationship to an exhibition in two or three sentences.
3. **Specifications / Spezifikationen**  
   Make size, material, edition, production, price, delivery, rights and technical requirements scannable.

#### Writing rules / Schreibregeln

- Name the object in the first sentence. Do not make readers pass through a manifesto to discover what is for sale.
- Use sensory language only when it is supported by a visible or material quality.
- State whether images show the exact item or a representative example.
- Use **limited edition** / „limitierte Edition“ only when a fixed edition size exists.
- State edition size in a consistent form: **Edition of 25 + 2 AP** / **Auflage: 25 + 2 AP**.
- Explain whether an item is signed, numbered, framed, unique, made to order or supplied with a certificate.
- Give dimensions as height × width × depth and state the unit.
- Separate product dimensions from packaged dimensions.
- State whether VAT and shipping are included where the shop requires it.
- Avoid investment language, future-value claims and suggestions of guaranteed rarity or returns.
- For digital works, distinguish the artwork, its file or display format and any associated token. State chain, wallet, licence and transfer conditions only when relevant and verified.
- Never imply that purchasing a token transfers copyright unless the terms explicitly say so.
- Do not call merchandise an artwork unless the artist and sales context define it as one.

| Dimensions / Maße | Edition size / Auflage |

### 3.4 Short formats / Kurzformate

#### Project card / Projektkarte

- **Title / Titel:** official project title
- **Label / Kennzeichnung:** Exhibition, Event or Edition / Ausstellung, Event oder Edition
- **Description / Beschreibung:** one factual sentence, ideally 12–24 words
- **Metadata / Metadaten:** date or year · venue/city or availability

**EN example**

> Fourteen international artists bring the network into the gallery through generative, moving-image and digital works.

**DE example**

> 14 internationale Künstler:innen bringen das Netzwerk mit generativen, filmischen und digitalen Arbeiten in den Galerieraum.

Use this example only when the media description has been verified.

#### Social caption / Social-Media-Caption

1. Lead with one concrete image, fact or question.
2. Give the essential context in one short paragraph.
3. Put date, place and action on separate lines.
4. Credit artists and photographers.
5. Use no more than three useful hashtags; omit them when they add no discovery value.

### 3.5 Final editorial check / Abschließender Redaktionscheck

Before publication, the editor confirms:

Vor der Veröffentlichung bestätigt die Redaktion:

- [ ] The opening names the actual subject; it does not begin with generic enthusiasm.
- [ ] All names, preferred spellings, pronouns, titles and diacritics are correct.
- [ ] Dates, times, venue, city, prices, dimensions, availability and links are verified.
- [ ] Curatorial, production, partner and funding roles are credited accurately.
- [ ] English uses UK spelling and local date/time formats.
- [ ] German is idiomatic and not a sentence-by-sentence translation.
- [ ] Both language versions contain the same facts and strength of claim.
- [ ] The tense matches the project status: upcoming, current or archive.
- [ ] Technical or art-specific terms are necessary and clear from context.
- [ ] Abstract claims are supported by a concrete work, medium, action or fact.
- [ ] The text contains no unsupported superlatives, hype or false urgency.
- [ ] Artist lists are complete and follow the agreed order.
- [ ] Calls to action describe what actually happens next.
- [ ] Access, language, duration, price and booking information are visible where relevant.
- [ ] Images have purposeful alt text and complete credits.
- [ ] Headings use sentence case, and the page contains no duplicate H1.

### 3.6 The 30-second ratata test / Der 30-Sekunden-ratata-Test

Read the copy once without the images. A finished text should answer:

Den Text einmal ohne Bilder lesen. Ein fertiger Text beantwortet:

1. **What is it? / Was ist es?**
2. **What is specific about it? / Was ist daran konkret und eigen?**
3. **Why is ratata presenting it? / Warum zeigt ratata es?**
4. **What can the reader do next? / Was kann das Publikum als Nächstes tun?**

If the answers are hidden behind mood, jargon or promotion, edit once more.

Wenn die Antworten hinter Stimmung, Fachsprache oder Werbung verschwinden, wird noch einmal überarbeitet.
